# Product

## Register

product

## Users

QuorumMind is for engineers, technical leads, and architecture reviewers who need to choose between competing implementation paths. They are usually working under delivery pressure, limited team capacity, and incomplete information. Their job is to turn a fuzzy architecture question into a defendable recommendation, a visible risk ledger, and an ADR that can be shared with the team.

## Product Purpose

QuorumMind runs a structured multi-agent decision room: independent proposals, blind critique, revisions, consensus scoring, risk analysis, and ADR generation. The product should help users understand why a recommendation won, where dissent remains, and which assumptions must be revisited later. Success means the user can trust the final decision without losing sight of trade-offs.

## Brand Personality

Clear, rigorous, composed.

The product should feel like a senior architecture review room: calm enough to think, dense enough to be useful, and opinionated enough to feel memorable as a portfolio project.

## Anti-references

Do not make QuorumMind look like a generic dark AI dashboard, a cyberpunk control room, or a decorative SaaS landing page. Avoid excessive glass panels, purple-blue neon gradients, giant hero typography, repeated card grids, meaningless metrics, and motion that delays the user's work.

## Design Principles

1. Decision first: the strongest visual weight belongs to the question, recommendation, dissent, and risk.
2. Show the reasoning path: proposal, critique, revision, score, and ADR should read as one traceable workflow.
3. Earn trust through restraint: use consistent components, clear contrast, and practical density.
4. Make dissent visible: disagreement is not an error state; it is a signal to investigate.
5. Keep the interface bilingual-ready: static product UI should switch cleanly between English and Chinese.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, focus visibility, semantic structure, and touch targets. Motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. The layout should remain usable on laptop, tablet, and mobile widths without horizontal scrolling.
